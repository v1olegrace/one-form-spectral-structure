## Question

Note E4, Section 10.6, estimates the O(alpha^5) three-photon term from the
two-loop correction to gamma gamma -> gamma gamma, the factor
1 + (186685/17514)(alpha/pi) of Dicus-Kao-Repko, note [10]. That factor
weights the two Euler-Heisenberg invariants as the gamma gamma cross section
does. The three-photon density weights them differently. R8 computes the
three-photon correction itself.

## Why the two-loop Euler-Heisenberg coefficients fix it

At O(alpha^5) and below 4m^2, the three-photon cut of the 1PI self-energy is
2 Re(M1* M2): M1 is the one-loop gamma* -> 3 gamma vertex, M2 the two-loop one.

- Every cut of the two-loop four-photon function passes through the fermion
  loop, so M2 is analytic below 4m^2 and its expansion in momenta starts
  with the constant-field (Euler-Heisenberg) quartic term.
- Diagrams with two fermion loops joined by a fourth photon vanish by Furry's
  theorem (five attachments on each loop).
- A vacuum-polarization insertion on a cut photon leaves the residue at
  k^2 = 0 equal to 1 in the on-shell scheme; its continuum starts at 4m^2.
- An insertion on the external leg is not 1PI.
- Operators that vanish on shell carry more derivatives and enter at higher
  order in s, as at one loop.

## Predictions, written before computing

1. The primary source gives, in L = c1 (E^2-B^2)^2 + c2 (E.B)^2,
   c1 -> c1 (1 + (40/9) alpha/pi) and c2 -> c2 (1 + (1315/252) alpha/pi).

## Results

**The coefficients** (`.agent/evidence/R8-literature.json`).

- Dunne, hep-th/0406216, eq. (4.11), quoting Ritus:
  L2 = (e^6/64 pi^4 m^4)[(16/81) X^2 + (263/162) Y^2]. Against eq. (1.9),
  2 alpha^2/(45 m^4)[X^2 + 7 Y^2], this gives 40/9 and 1315/252.
- Ravndal, hep-ph/9708449, Section 3, states the same two numbers.
- Two checks from independent computations quoted in the same review:
  - The magnetic series, eq. (4.15), gives 40/9.
  - The self-dual closed forms, eqs. (6.2) and (6.6), give 25/4 for
    4 c1 - c2. A self-dual field measures that combination.
    (4·40/9 - 7·1315/252)/(4 - 7) = 25/4. With 40/9 from the magnetic case,
    this fixes 1315/252.
- Ritus's own papers were not read.

**The weights** (exact rational arithmetic, `three_photon_window.py`).

- With a = c1/4 and b = c2/16, the (FF)^2 and (FF~)^2 coefficients:
  - sigma(gamma gamma) is proportional to 2(a-b)^2 + (a+b)^2 at every angle.
    A spinor-helicity count gives the same weights: all-plus amplitudes go as
    a-b, mixed ones as a+b.
  - rho_3gamma is proportional to 5(a-b)^2 + 3(a+b)^2.
  - The one-loop values recover 139 and 17.
- kappa_gamma_gamma = 25555/2502 = 10.214.
- kappa_3gamma = 1555/153 = 10.163. The leading coefficient grows by 2.36%.

**DKR note [10].** Write kappa = 2 d2 + w (d1 - d2).

- The rate gives w = 40/139, and our value follows.
- With w = -40/139 the expression equals their 186685/17514 exactly.
- So their factor is ours with one sign reversed. Their calculation is not
  available.

## Predictions against results

1. **Confirmed.** 40/9 and 1315/252.
2. **Failed.** The weights give 25555/2502, not 186685/17514. The
   discrepancy is diagnosed as a single sign reversal on DKR's side.
3. **Confirmed.** kappa = 10.163.
4. **Missed.** The shifts are 0.0125 to 0.0130, not 0.014 to 0.016. The
   prediction assumed DKR's 2.5% on the whole three-photon term. The computed
   factor is 2.36% and acts on the leading coefficient only, which carries
   about 87% of Phi_3gamma at the radii.
